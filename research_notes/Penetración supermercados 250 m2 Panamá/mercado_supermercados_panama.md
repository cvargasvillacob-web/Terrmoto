## 1. How big is Panama's grocery market, and how is it split between modern and traditional trade?

### Takeaway
No single authoritative 2025–2026 figure for total grocery sales turned up. Proxies cluster around **US$3.4–3.6 billion a year for supermarkets/packaged food**, and an older (≈2015–16) Mastercard estimate puts **another ~US$3.4 billion in ~49,000 minisúper/abarroterías**. Kantar-based data reported by Panamanian press gives **modern channels ~74% vs traditional ~25%** of consumer-goods spending, with the traditional channel slowly losing share (‑1.2 pts in the latest year cited).

### Cited Findings
**Market size (modern / packaged food)**
- Packaged-food retail sales were about **US$3.4 billion in 2024** (Euromonitor), forecast to reach **US$4.5 billion by 2029**. A separate FAS/Food Export assessment projects **US$4.7 billion by 2028**, which is **+US$1 billion (+27.1%) vs 2024**. — [Food Export Association, Panama Market Assessment 2024](https://foodexport.org/wp-content/uploads/Panama-Market-Assessment-2024.pdf); [Euromonitor, Staple Foods in Panama](https://www.euromonitor.com/staple-foods-in-panama/report). The search summary blended both sources, so check which figure belongs to which.
- ACOVIPA (Asociación de Comerciantes y Distribuidores de Víveres y Similares de Panamá) said in July 2026 that Panama has **more than 250 established supermarkets** and that the supermarket chain **contributes more than US$3,600 million to GDP**. ACOVIPA said this while objecting to the government's Tiendas del Pueblo. — [La Estrella de Panamá, jul‑2026](https://www.laestrella.com.pa/economia/acovipa-cuestiona-las-tiendas-del-pueblo-el-ima-rechaza-competencia-desleal-HA24061341)
- Grocery e-commerce was projected to reach **US$77.5 million in 2025**, per USDA FAS GAIN Retail Foods Annual PN2025‑0009 (30 Sep 2025). — [USDA FAS, Panama Retail Foods Annual 2025](https://www.fas.usda.gov/data/gain/2025/09/panama-retail-foods-annual). Another estimate is **US$75.6 million in 2025, = 6.3% of total e-commerce** in Panama. — [ecommercedb.com, Grocery market in Panama](https://ecommercedb.com/markets/pa/food)
- USDA FAS calls Panama "the most modern retail economy in the region, with a robust eCommerce marketplace and food delivery platforms". It says supermarkets, hypermarkets and independent specialty chains dominate grocery, and that competition is "based primarily on price and convenience". — [USDA FAS, Panama Retail Foods Annual](https://www.fas.usda.gov/data/panama-retail-foods-annual); [USDA FAS 2025](https://www.fas.usda.gov/data/gain/2025/09/panama-retail-foods-annual)
- A newer USDA GAIN **Retail Foods Annual PN2026‑0013 (2 Oct 2026)** exists. Its abstract ranks Panama **31st** among top global markets for U.S. agricultural exports, down from 23rd in the 2025 edition. — [USDA FAS, Panama Retail Foods Annual 2026](https://www.fas.usda.gov/data/gain/2026/10/panama-retail-foods-annual). U.S. agricultural and related exports to Panama were **≈US$997.9 million in 2025**. — [USDA FAS Exporter Guide Annual, jul‑2026](https://www.fas.usda.gov/data/gain/2026/07/panama-exporter-guide-annual)
- A market-research vendor values Panama "convenience food" at **US$758.4 million in 2025**, with supermarkets and hypermarkets the leading channel at **39.8%**. This source is low-reliability and covers a narrow category. — [Strategic Revenue Insights](https://www.strategicrevenueinsights.com/industry/panama-convenience-food-market)

**Modern vs traditional split**
- In Kantar data reported by Revista Summa, **modern channels (hypermarkets and supermarkets) hold 74%** and the **traditional channel 25%**, with **supermarkets at 93% within modern trade**. — [Revista Summa](https://revistasumma.com/?p=227646). La Estrella reported the same numbers the other way round: "supermarkets lead with 74%" and "modern channels concentrate 93% of grocery sales". It also says the **traditional channel (minisúper, tiendas de barrio) lost 1.2 points in the last year**. — [La Estrella de Panamá, "Supermercados y marcas privadas…"](https://www.laestrella.com.pa/economia/supermercados-y-marcas-privadas-la-tendencia-de-consumo-en-panama-EK9804161). **These two readings conflict, and neither snippet showed the study year.** Summa's reading (74% modern / 25% traditional) is the more internally consistent one.
- Private label is **5% of total consumption**, up **43% in value over 5 years**. — [La Estrella](https://www.laestrella.com.pa/economia/supermercados-y-marcas-privadas-la-tendencia-de-consumo-en-panama-EK9804161); [Revista Summa](https://revistasumma.com/?p=227646)
- Across Central America, Kantar says the traditional channel is **losing ground (‑1%)** because shoppers see its prices as high and its assortment as limited. — [Kantar Worldpanel CAM, Aprendizajes 2024 y Oportunidades 2025](https://www.kantar.com/latin-america/Inspiracion/Consumidor/2025-Worldpanel-CAM-Aprendizajes-2024-y-Oportunidades-2025)

**Traditional trade (abarroterías / "chinitos" / minisúper)**
- A Mastercard study found Panama's **minisúper and abarroterías sell about US$3,400 million a year**, through about **49,000 such stores**, and **fewer than 1% accepted electronic payments**. The total food and beverage market was ~**US$5,000 million in 2015**. **The article is undated but probably from ~2015–2016.** — [Revista E&N / La Prensa](https://www.revistaeyn.com/centroamericaymundo/panama-minisuper-y-abarroterias-con-ventas-de-3400-millones-al-ano-LVEN1067396)
- A 2017 USDA GAIN report put the "traditional" market at **over 35,000 mom & pop stores in Panama City**, selling small pack sizes. — [USDA GAIN Food Processing Ingredients, dic‑2017](https://apps.fas.usda.gov/newgainapi/api/report/downloadreportbyfilename?filename=Food+Processing+Ingredients_Panama+City_Panama_12-29-2017.pdf)
- A 2020-era Panamá América article cited **"more than 14 thousand abarroterías" nationwide**. — [Panamá América, 2020 (via search summary)](https://www.panamaamerica.com.pa/economia/coronavirus-en-panama-acodeco-trata-de-acorralar-la-especulacion-en-los-minisuper-1159567). This is far below the Mastercard 49,000, probably because definitions differ.
- A La Prensa opinion piece says Chinese-Panamanian merchants expanded into communities outside the capital and now run "**miles de mercados identificados como 'minisúper'**". — [La Prensa, "De abarrotería a minisúper"](https://www.prensa.com/impresa/opinion/de-abarroteria-a-minisuper/)
- La Estrella reports that **abarroterías and minisúper keep the Canasta Básica Familiar more expensive than supermarkets do**. — [La Estrella de Panamá](https://www.laestrella.com.pa/economia/091028/basica-canasta-chinitos-encarecen)
- An older (≈2011) case study said **five local capital groups held >90% of the formal supermarket market**. — [CLADEA case "El futuro de Rey Holdings Corp."](https://cladea2014.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/10/paper_310.pdf). Another source says Panama's wider retail is fragmented, with **7–8 big players holding only ~45% of sales** (undated). — [Estrategia & Negocios](https://estrategiaynegocios.net/lasclavesdeldia/1231655-330/as%C3%AD-funciona-la-competencia-de-grupo-rey-en-retail-en-panam%C3%A1)

### Inferences
- A reasonable working size for total at-home food and grocery retail is **~US$6–7.5 billion**: roughly US$3.5–4 billion in supermarkets and clubs (ACOVIPA's US$3.6 billion plus Euromonitor's packaged food) and roughly US$2–3.5 billion in traditional stores. The traditional figure scales the old Mastercard US$3.4 billion down for share lost since then. **This is a triangulation, not a sourced number.**
- The traditional channel is still roughly **a quarter of spending** and is spread across **tens of thousands of outlets** (14,000–49,000 depending on definition). That fragmented, higher-priced, cash-heavy base is what a modern 250 m² proximity format would take share from.

### Gaps
- No 2024–2026 total grocery market size (USD) from INEC, Euromonitor or Nielsen was found. INEC retail-trade (comercio al por menor) value-added series were not retrieved.
- No current census of abarroterías or minisúper was found (Contraloría/INEC "Directorio de Establecimientos" or MICI were not reached).
- The Kantar 74/25 split has no year attached, and the two outlets contradict each other on which figure is which.
- The full USDA PN2025‑0009 and PN2026‑0013 reports could not be opened (host blocked).

## 2. Who are the main chains, how many stores does each have, and what are their market shares?

### Takeaway
The formal market is concentrated in four groups:
- **Super 99** (Importadora Ricamar / Martinelli family): ~51+ stores.
- **Grupo Rey** (73.45% owned by Corporación Favorita, Ecuador): 174 points of sale across all banners, including pharmacies, with 35 Rey stores.
- **Supermercados Xtra**: 62 stores at end‑2025, with Fitch estimating ~27% share of sales in 2024.
- **PriceSmart**: 7 clubs.

These are followed by El Machetazo (18 stores), Super Carnes (~14–15), Riba Smith (~8), El Fuerte (~5), Felipe Motta (~15 wine/deli) and the state-run IMA Tiendas del Pueblo (14 → 21). No recent audited market-share table by chain was found.

### Inferences
- Xtra's sales area divided by its store count (125,000 m² / 54 stores) gives an **average of ~2,300 m² per store** (Fitch, 2024). Big boxes are typical across the market: Machetazo 18,000 m², MAX Super Carnes 6,000 m², Super Mr. Precio 1,400 m². **None of the leading chains' core formats is in the 150–400 m² range.**
- Adding up identifiable supermarket banners gives roughly 200–230 stores: Super 99 ~51–58, Rey 35, Xtra 62, Machetazo 18, Super Carnes 15, Riba Smith ~8, El Fuerte ~5, PriceSmart 7, plus Romero and Mr. Precio. That fits ACOVIPA's figure of "**>250 supermarkets**".
- La Chorrera and Panamá Oeste are now hotly contested: B‑sí's first store (August 2026) and Machetazo #18 (October 2026) both opened there.

### Gaps
- **No current chain-level market shares** were found, apart from Fitch's ~27% for Xtra (2024) and Rey's >30% from 2011.
- Exact current store counts are missing for Super 99 (the latest verifiable figure is 51), Romero, Mr. Precio, ZAZ, Riba Smith and El Fuerte.
- No data were found on Supermercados Jumbo, Super Kosher, Méga Depot, Super Barú or other regional chains in Chiriquí, Azuero and Colón.
- No revenue figures were found for Super 99 or Xtra. Xtra's Latinex/SMV financial statements are probably available but were not reached.

## 3. Who already runs small, express or convenience formats, and who is entering?

### Takeaway
The **150–400 m² modern grocery space is thin**. It contains:
- **Grupo Rey's ZAZ Food Store** convenience chain (store count unknown);
- **gas-station convenience stores** such as Terpel (46 stores in August 2026), Puma and Delta;
- Rey's **Mr. Precio** soft-discount (historically ~500 m²);
- the **newest entrant, Favorita's B‑sí discount format** (first Panama store in La Chorrera, August 2026; second being fitted out near Plaza Las Américas).

**No evidence** was found of Oxxo, D1, Ara, Tiendas 3B, Justo & Bueno, or "Express" banners from Super 99, Xtra, Rey or Riba Smith operating in Panama.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- **B‑sí is the most direct threat** to a ~250 m² proximity concept. It is a discount format backed by the market's best-funded retailer, with Grupo Rey's logistics behind it (US$58 million BID Invest financing). If it scales, it will most likely target the same lower-middle-income dense neighborhoods.
- Gas-station stores (Terpel's 46, plus Puma and Delta) serve impulse and convenience purchases, not fill-in grocery. ZAZ is premium convenience rather than a grocery assortment. That leaves room for a **fresh-led, grocery-assortment 250 m² "super de barrio" with chain prices**, a positioning none of the big four visibly occupies.

## 4. How do Panamanian consumers shop, pay and respond to prices, and what price controls apply?

### Takeaway
Shoppers are **price-squeezed and trading down**: 31% of households struggle to cover basic monthly expenses, the average ticket fell 6.1%, visit frequency is falling among low-income groups, and bulk buying and private label are growing. **Digital payments are mainstream** through Yappy (~1.5–1.7 million users, ~36,000 merchants, ~70 million transactions a month). Grocery e-commerce is still small (~US$77 million in 2025), but quick commerce is a strategic push. **Food price controls (in place since 2014) were allowed to lapse in September 2026.** By then they covered only 4 products.

### Cited Findings
**Spending, frequency and ticket**
- In 2025 Kantar found **31% of Panamanian households struggle to cover basic monthly expenses**, and linked this pressure to a **6.1% drop in the average purchase ticket**. — [Kantar, 2025](https://www.kantar.com/latin-america/inspiracion/consumidor/2025/tres-de-cada-10-hogares-panamenos-batallan-para-cubrir-sus-gastos-mensuales)
- Worldpanel by Numerator says low socioeconomic strata saw **falling purchase frequency, especially in Panama and El Salvador**. In Central America to **June 2026**, **frequency fell 5% and the average ticket rose 5%**. — [E&N, "Hogares centroamericanos reducen 4% su consumo"](https://www.revistaeyn.com/empresasymanagement/hogares-centroamericanos-reducen-4-su-consumo-ante-presion-de-precios-JE32272083); [Kantar, nuevo mapa del consumo CAM](https://www.kantar.com/latin-america/inspiracion/consumidor/2025/el-nuevo-mapa-del-consumo-en-centroamerica)
- At end‑2024 frequency was declining and **the traditional channel was hit hardest**. Panama and Costa Rica were described as the more resilient markets. — [Kantar CAM 2024/2025](https://www.kantar.com/latin-america/Inspiracion/Consumidor/2025-Worldpanel-CAM-Aprendizajes-2024-y-Oportunidades-2025)

### Inferences
- Low-income consumers buy less often but at higher tickets, and they buy more bulk and private label. That favors a proximity format with **sharp prices on a short list of KVIs** (rice, oil, milk, chicken, bread), a **private-label basket**, and **Yappy and card acceptance**, a clear difference from cash-heavy abarroterías.
- With controls lapsed, a new entrant has **margin freedom on staples**. But the state's low-price channels (IMA fairs, Tiendas del Pueblo, Panamá Pa' Ti) remain a price benchmark in lower-income areas.

## 5. Macroeconomic context 2024–2026 (GDP, inflation, unemployment, Cobre Panamá, canal, 9 Oct 2026 earthquake)

### Takeaway
Growth is solid: **GDP +4.4% in 2025**, IMAE +4.9% in January–May 2026, and the IMF projects **~5% for 2026**. Inflation is near zero, from ‑0.02% y/y in October 2025 to 1.3% by May 2026, with the IMF expecting a little over 2% for 2026. But **unemployment is high, ~10.4% at end‑2025**, and **Cobre Panamá remains closed** (since November 2023). The **9 October 2026 M7.7 earthquake** (epicenter in Azuero) closed many shops in **Chitré, Los Santos and Tonosí** temporarily. Supermarkets reportedly reopened quickly, but agro-industry (beef, milk) and MSMEs in Azuero were hit.

## 6. What rules apply to opening small supermarkets (Aviso de Operación, zoning, hours, alcohol, foreign ownership / Article 293)?

### Takeaway
Opening a store is relatively light-touch:
- a sworn **Aviso de Operación** filed online through **Panamá Emprende** (Ley 5 de 2007);
- municipal land-use, building and alcohol permits on top, with supermarkets and minisúper in **alcohol license "Nivel 1"** (Ley 54 de 2013) when liquor is not the main activity.

The binding constraint for foreign capital is **Article 293 of the Constitution**. It reserves **retail trade (comercio al por menor)** for Panamanians by birth, qualifying naturalized citizens, pre‑1972 foreign retailers, and **legal entities owned by those persons**. Wholesale (Article 294) is open.

### Cited Findings
- **Article 293** (as described in Panama's FTA reservation schedules): only these may conduct retail trade: (a) Panamanians by birth; (b) naturalized persons, after a **3‑year waiting period** per the FTA schedule, while other sources cite >10 years of residence; (c) foreigners who legally ran retail businesses before the 1972 Constitution; and (d) **juridical persons, Panamanian or foreign, only if owned by persons in those categories**. "Retail" covers **(a) sale of goods to the consumer and (b) representation or agency of producing or commercial firms**. — [USTR, US‑Panama TPA Annex I (Retail Sales)](https://ustr.gov/archive/assets/Trade_Agreements/Bilateral/Panama_FTA/Final_Text/asset_upload_file913_10381.pdf); [EFTA Annex XVIII App. 2](https://www.efta.int/sites/default/files/media/documents/legal-texts/free-trade-relations/central-america/annexes-en/annex-xviii-appendix-2-panama.pdf); [MICI, Anexo I Lista de Panamá](https://mici.gob.pa/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/10-Anexo-I-Lista-de-Panamá.pdf); [Limitless Legal](https://limitlesslegal.com/en-us/blog/how-to-start-a-retail-business-in-panama-as-a-foreigner)
- **Exceptions:** sales to a commercial company are not retail even if it is the end user; **services are not retail**; non-authorized foreigners **may take part in companies that sell products they manufacture themselves**; **wholesale (Article 294) is open** but may be restricted by law to protect Panamanian wholesalers. — [Mondaq, "Retail Business and the Free Trade Treaty"](https://www.mondaq.com/international-trade-investment/38774/retail-business-and-the-free-trade-treaty); [iurispa 2026 guide](https://iurispa.com/en/guides/restricted-professions-and-activities-for-foreigners-in-panama/); [Constitute Project, Panama 1972 rev. 2004](https://www.constituteproject.org/constitution/Panama_2004)
- In practice, La Prensa notes that the rule led to **informal workarounds such as marrying Panamanian nationals** to run shops, and that it has slowed retail capital and technology inflows. — [La Prensa, opinion](https://www.prensa.com/impresa/opinion/de-abarroteria-a-minisuper/). USDA FAS (2022) likewise notes that the Constitution **prohibits foreigners from owning retail stores**. — [USDA FAS search summary](https://www.fas.usda.gov/data/panama-retail-foods-annual)
- **Numbering caveat:** Justia's online copy places the retail rule at a different article number than 293. Treaty annexes and the press cite 293. Verify against the official Gaceta Oficial text. — [Justia Panamá](https://panama.justia.com/federales/constitucion-politica-de-la-republica-de-panama/titulo-x/)
- **Aviso de Operación:** this is filed through **Panamá Emprende** under **Ley 5 de 2007** and is a **sworn declaration** with criminal liability for false statements. The law bars adding requirements without an express legal basis. — [Ley 5 de 2007 (Justia PDF)](https://docs.panama.justia.com/federales/leyes/5-de-2007-jan-12-2007.pdf); [Gaceta Oficial](https://www.gacetaoficial.gob.pa/pdfTemp/25709/2494.pdf). The vice minister of commerce warned that **businesses without a valid Aviso will be sanctioned or closed**. — [Panamá América](https://panamaamerica.com.pa/economia/comercios-seran-sancionados-o-cerrados-si-no-cumplen-con-el-aviso-de-operacion-advierte). MICI is **automating the closure of avisos** to stop undue municipal charges in Panama City (2026). — [Panamá América](https://www.panamaamerica.com.pa/economia/mici-automatiza-el-cierre-de-avisos-de-operacion-para-evitar-cobros-indebidos-en-el-municipio-de-panama-1282045). "Fondas" lead new Aviso filings. — [Panamá América](https://www.panamaamerica.com.pa/sociedad/fondas-lideran-avisos-de-operacion-en-panama-1249934)
- **Alcohol:** **Ley 54 de 2013** (amending Ley 55 de 1973 and Ley 5 de 2007) puts **restaurants, cafeterías, supermarkets, minisúper, abarroterías and markets** where liquor is not the main activity in **Nivel 1**. Municipalities issue and revoke liquor permits. Panama City and San Miguelito authorities have complained that easy registration lets bars proliferate. — [CEPAL Plataforma Urbana](https://plataformaurbana.cepal.org/en/node/1224); [La Prensa, Locales de venta de licor bajo escrutinio](https://www.prensa.com/impresa/panorama/Locales-venta-licor-escrutinio_0_4504049595.html)
- A new **MSME law, Ley 451 of 31 October 2024 (AMPYME)**, exists. Its specific incentives for small retailers were not reviewed. — [DGI/MEF PDF](https://dgi.mef.gob.pa/LDRd/Leyes/Ley%20451%20de%2031%20de%20octubre%20de%202024%20AMPYME.pdf)

### Inferences
- Any foreign-backed 250 m² chain needs a **Panamanian-qualifying ownership structure for the retail operating company**, e.g., a majority-Panamanian partner or local holding. Wholesale, distribution, real estate, franchise or brand licensing, logistics and services can sit with foreign capital, since those are not "retail". Favorita's 73.45% of the Panama-listed Grupo Rey and PriceSmart's Panama clubs show foreign-led grocery retail operates in Panama, but **how they comply with Article 293 is not documented in the sources found**.
- Because minisúper and supermarkets fall in **Nivel 1**, beer and liquor can be sold as a secondary activity, an important traffic and margin driver for proximity formats. Municipal hours restrictions (e.g., ley seca on election days or curfews) must be checked district by district.

### Gaps
- **No source found** on how Corporación Favorita/Grupo Rey or PriceSmart legally structure around Article 293. Their SMV/Latinex prospectuses or the 10‑K risk factors would be the place to check.
- No information found on zoning categories for small commercial premises (MIVIOT/municipal "uso de suelo" codes), legal opening hours, or permitted alcohol sale hours by district.
- No information on Panamá Emprende fees or timelines, sanitary permits (MINSA), or fire-safety (Bomberos) permits for a 250 m² store.

## 7. Where is the white space for a ~250 m² proximity supermarket? (synthesis of the evidence above)

### Takeaway
The evidence points to a **gap between big-box chains (≥1,400 m², typically ~2,300 m²+) and the ~14,000–49,000 informal abarroterías/minisúper**. The formal 150–400 m² grocery tier is occupied only by ZAZ (convenience), gas-station stores and, since August 2026, Favorita's B‑sí (discount). Population growth is fastest in **Panamá Oeste**, where competition is now arriving. The most underserved demand appears to be **price-sensitive, digital-paying urban households** whose neighborhood shop is a higher-priced, cash-oriented abarrotería.

### Cited Findings
- Modern-trade boxes are large: Xtra averages ~2,300 m² (125,000 m² over 54 stores, Fitch 2024); Machetazo #18 is >18,000 m²; MAX Super Carnes is 6,000 m²; Super Mr. Precio is 1,400 m². — [Panorama Económico/Fitch](https://panoramaeconomicopma.com/2024/09/fitch-ratings-reafirma-calificacion-apan-para-supermercados-xtra-con-perspectiva-estable/); [Telemetro](https://www.telemetro.com/contenido-patrocinado/el-machetazo-inaugura-su-sucursal-no-18-la-chorrera-n6095214); [Panamá América](https://panamaamerica.com.pa/provincias/el-nuevo-max-super-carnes-abre-sus-puertas-en-san-antonio-veraguas-1237245); [newsinamerica](https://newsinamerica.com/pdcc/boletin/2024/llega-el-primer-super-mr-precio-a-panama/)
- Kantar says the traditional channel is losing share because of **perceived high prices and limited assortment**. — [Kantar CAM](https://www.kantar.com/latin-america/Inspiracion/Consumidor/2025-Worldpanel-CAM-Aprendizajes-2024-y-Oportunidades-2025). Abarroterías keep the Canasta Básica costlier than supermarkets. — [La Estrella](https://www.laestrella.com.pa/economia/091028/basica-canasta-chinitos-encarecen)
- Panamá Oeste has the fastest population growth: 2.7% a year intercensal and 1.8% a year projected for 2023–35. — [Panamá América](https://panamaamerica.com.pa/provincias/la-poblacion-de-panama-oeste-aumento-506-segun-el-inec-1224907). **B‑sí #1 (August 2026) and Machetazo #18 (October 2026) both opened in La Chorrera.** — [Primicias](https://www.primicias.ec/economia/empresas/tiendas-bsi-descuento-supermercado-supermaxi-corporacion-favorita-panama-130788/); [Telemetro](https://www.telemetro.com/contenido-patrocinado/el-machetazo-inaugura-su-sucursal-no-18-la-chorrera-n6095214)
- The government's low-price network is expanding into secondary towns such as Río Hato, Aguadulce, Soná, David and Puerto Armuelles. — [Telemetro](https://www.telemetro.com/nacionales/ima-proyecta-aumentar-14-21-las-tiendas-del-pueblo-panama-n6081217)

### Inferences
- **Candidate white-space zones** (inference, to be validated with geo-data): dense mid- to low-income corridors in **Panama City's east and north (San Miguelito, Tocumen/24 de Diciembre, Las Garzas, Pacora, Juan Díaz)** and **Panamá Oeste (Arraiján, La Chorrera, Vista Alegre)**, where chains run few, large stores. The big four are building hypermarkets and large boxes here (Hiper Rey Nuevo Tocumen, Super 99 Las Garzas, Machetazo La Chorrera), which leaves **walk-in, daily fill-in trips to abarroterías**.
- **Interior cities** (Santiago, David, Chitré, Penonomé, Aguadulce) have chain coverage by Super 99, Rey/Romero, Xtra and Super Carnes but fewer proximity options. **Azuero after the earthquake** is a contrarian opportunity: independent stock and premises were damaged. It is also a risk because of demand shock and seismic exposure.
- **Competitive response risk:** Grupo Rey (B‑sí, ZAZ, Mr. Precio) is the incumbent best placed to scale a small format quickly. Super 99 and Xtra have not shown small formats, but have balance sheets that could fund them; Xtra raised US$95 million in October 2026.

### Gaps
- No geospatial store-density data (stores per 10,000 inhabitants by corregimiento) was found. It is needed to rank micro-markets.
- No rent benchmarks for 200–300 m² ground-floor retail, and no 250 m² store P&L benchmarks for Panama, were found.
